MAO rents out material, engineers temporary works, plans pours in 3D, and sends supervisors to site. Here is what each of those involves.

Service 01

Equipment Hire

MAO rents out scaffolding, formwork, and falsework from stockyards in Dammam, Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai, and Cairo. The fleet sits at 150,000+ tons, inspected and tagged before dispatch. Short-term and long-term contracts are available, including rent-to-buy.

What goes out the door: Profex wall panels, Ringlock scaffolding, Cuplock frames, Shorbrace towers, Manar slab tables, European props, steel walers, H20 beams, and all the tie rods, clamps, and boards to go with them. One delivery, one lot.

  • ■ Profex, Ringlock, Cuplock, Shorbrace, Manar Table, European Props, H20 Beams, Steel Boards, Tie Rods
  • ■ Short-term, long-term, and rent-to-buy terms
  • ■ Every piece cleaned, reconditioned, and load-tested before leaving the yard

Service 02

Structural Engineering

The MAO engineering department writes the calculation packages for temporary works: load charts, tie-rod checks, foundation reactions, and wind stability. Calculations follow DIN 18218, DIN 4420, BS EN 12810/12811, and Saudi Building Code.

Work is done in-house and reviewed by a chartered structural engineer. The department also prepares technical submittals for consultant approval and can fast-track PE-stamped drawings when the programme is tight.

  • ■ Static load calculations, tie-rod tension checks, foundation reactions
  • ■ Wind and dynamic load analysis for climbing systems and falsework
  • ■ Value engineering to cut tonnage without reducing safety factors
  • ■ Technical submittals and direct liaison with consultant teams

Service 03

BIM Planning and Formwork Layout

The BIM team builds 3D models of formwork layouts inside Navisworks and Revit. Clashes between rebar, embeds, and formwork panels are caught before anything reaches site. Pour sequences are planned floor by floor, with color-coded erection drawings for the site crew.

Output includes detailed BOQs, material delivery schedules, and step-by-step assembly drawings. The goal is fewer surprises during the pour and faster cycle times between strips.

  • ■ Parametric 3D BIM models in Navisworks and Revit
  • ■ Clash detection between formwork, rebar, and embeds
  • ■ Pour cycle optimization and color-coded erection sequences
  • ■ Material take-offs and delivery schedules per pour

Service 04

Site Supervision and After-Sales

MAO places field engineers on site during the erection and striking of formwork and scaffolding. They check that the installation matches the approved drawings, run pre-pour structural sign-offs, and coordinate with consultant inspection teams.

Toolbox talks cover safe handling, cycle speed, and common mistakes. When something goes wrong at short notice, the field team responds to keep the pour on programme.

  • ■ On-site supervision during erection, use, and dismantling
  • ■ Pre-pour checklists and formal sign-offs before concrete placement
  • ■ Toolbox training for site crews on handling and cycle speed
  • ■ Emergency technical response for urgent pour schedules
